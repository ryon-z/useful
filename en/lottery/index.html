The 24% Federal Withholding Trap: Why Winners Owe Millions in April

When an American citizen or resident wins a major lottery jackpot exceeding $5,000, federal law requires lottery administrators to immediately withhold 24% for the Internal Revenue Service (IRS).

The Tax Reality Breakdown:

Any income exceeding $609,350 (Single) or $731,200 (Married Filing Jointly) falls squarely into the top federal marginal tax bracket of 37%. Since multimillion-dollar jackpots exceed this threshold almost immediately, the winner is responsible for the full 37% tax rate on the entire prize.

Result: On a $500 Million cash lump sum, the lottery withholds $120 Million (24%), but the winner still owes approximately $65 Million (the remaining 13%) when filing federal taxes the following April!

US State Lottery Tax Rates (Best & Worst States for Winners)

Where you buy your ticket and where you reside dramatically influences your final take-home fortune. State tax rates range from 0% to 10.9%.

Best States (0% State Tax on Lottery)

  • California: State Lottery winnings exempt by law (Cal. Rev. & Tax Code § 17082).
  • Texas, Florida, Washington, Tennessee: No state personal income tax.
  • South Dakota, Wyoming, Alaska, Nevada: No state income tax.
  • Delaware, Pennsylvania: State lottery prizes exempt from state income tax.

Highest State Lottery Tax Rates

  • New York: 10.9% state tax + 3.876% NYC resident tax (up to 14.776% total local).
  • New Jersey: 10.75% top state bracket.
  • District of Columbia (DC): 10.75%.
  • Oregon: 9.9% state withholding.
  • Minnesota: 9.85%.
  • Maryland: 8.95% (residents) / 8.0% (non-residents).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q. Should I choose the Lump Sum Cash Option or the 30-Year Annuity?
Over 90% of past lottery jackpot winners choose the lump-sum cash option because it grants immediate control over the capital, allowing investments in diversified assets, index funds, or real estate. However, the 30-year annuity provides guaranteed, inflation-adjusted (5% compounding increase per year) income, protecting winners from financial mismanagement and sudden wealth bankruptcies.
Q. Can non-US citizens or tourists claim Powerball and Mega Millions prizes?
Yes. Non-US residents are eligible to win and claim prizes as long as tickets were legally purchased within a participating US jurisdiction. However, federal withholding for non-resident aliens is typically 30% upfront (instead of 24%), and bilateral tax treaties between the US and the winner's home country will govern additional liabilities.
Q. What happens to the remaining annuity payments if the winner dies?
Contrary to common myth, remaining annuity payments do not vanish or revert back to the state. The remaining stream of annual payments becomes part of the winner's legal estate and passes to designated beneficiaries or heirs.
