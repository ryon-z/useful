US Tipping Etiquette & Standard Rates (2026 Guide)

Tipping in the United States is deeply woven into the compensation structure of service hospitality workers. Under federal labor law, the tipped minimum wage is significantly lower than standard minimum wage in many states, making tips an essential component of servers' livelihoods.

Full-Service Restaurants

  • 15%: Acceptable for baseline or minimal service.
  • 18% – 20%: Current standard benchmark for good, attentive service across the US.
  • 22% – 25%: Customary for outstanding service, high-end fine dining, or large parties.

Bars & Coffee Shops

  • Beer & Wine: $1 to $2 per draft or bottle poured.
  • Cocktails: $2 to $3 per mixed/craft cocktail.
  • Open Tabs: 18% to 20% of the entire final bar check.
  • Baristas: $1 per handcrafted espresso or drink order.

Food Delivery & Services

  • Food Delivery (UberEats, DoorDash): 15%–20% (minimum $3–$5 for rain, snow, or distant trips).
  • Rideshare (Uber, Lyft): 15%–20% of fare.
  • Buffet Restaurants: 10% for drink refills and plate clearing.

Why You Should Tip on the Pre-Tax Amount

One of the most common questions among American diners and international travelers is whether tips should be calculated before or after local sales tax is added to the receipt.

The Mathematical Reality:

Etiquette experts (including the Emily Post Institute) confirm that tips should be calculated strictly on the subtotal of the food and beverage items ordered. Sales tax is a mandatory government levy that does not reflect restaurant hospitality or waitstaff performance.

In metropolitan areas with combined state and local sales taxes near 9% to 10% (such as New York City, Chicago, or Los Angeles), tipping on the post-tax bill unnecessarily inflates your tip by an extra 1.5% to 2.0% of your total check.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What does "Automatic Gratuity" mean on large party checks?
Many US restaurants automatically apply an 18% or 20% service fee or "Auto-Gratuity" for parties of 6 or more diners. When this line item appears on the bill, you do not need to add an additional tip unless service was extraordinary and you choose to do so voluntarily.
Q. Do I need to tip for takeout / to-go orders?
Tipping for takeout orders is strictly optional. While standard dine-in service commands 18%–20%, 0% to 10% is customary for counter pickup or to-go orders to reward kitchen packaging and hostess assembly, but there is no social obligation to leave a full 20% tip.
Q. How do I fairly split a bill when people ordered different amounts?
When orders are roughly comparable, equal N-way splitting is standard etiquette. However, if one person did not drink alcohol or ordered significantly less, calculate their food item plus tax and tip, and split the remaining balance equally among the other guests.
